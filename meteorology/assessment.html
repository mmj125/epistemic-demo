Meteorology & Climate Science · End-of-Unit Assessment

Investigate a real phenomenon

This assessment assigns you a real comparison pulled from the same CERES and ERA5 data you used in the investigation — not made-up numbers. It is not a memory test — you may use your notebook and everything you learned in the investigation. What matters is whether you can read real data and reason about it, including about how sure scientists actually are.

Before you begin

This is open-notes, not open-answer. You've been assigned one of several real comparisons at random. Your case may differ from a classmate's, so answers copied from someone else likely won't match your data.

Cite what the display actually shows. Vague claims ("it went up") earn less credit than specific ones (direction, rough magnitude, where in the region the pattern is strongest or weakest).

You are not told the name of the phenomenon. Part of what's being assessed is whether you can work out what's going on from the data and from what you already know about the energy budget, not whether you can recognize a vocabulary word.
