Purple pairs with the horizontal/vertical members, blue with the diagonals — the closer length match by a wide margin (~9-12% off vs. 3-6x worse cross-paired). Length, mass, and connector cost are from Matt's Decision Criteria spreadsheet and follow-up (Purple: 9.5cm / 0.9g, Blue: 13.5cm / 1.3g, connectors: 0.81g / $10 each). One connector per node that still has a member attached, regardless of how many members meet there — Matt gave one mass and price for all connectors, not a type-per-degree rule like the old standard-K'NEX sheet had.

Total cost adds rod length (cm) to connector price ($) as one number — that's not a dimensionally clean thing to do, but it's exactly what Matt's own spreadsheet does too (member cost = raw length, node cost = count × price, both summed into one "Total Construction Cost"), since there's still no real per-rod dollar price to use instead of length. Ported as-is for consistency with the source tool.

Matt's actual point with the $1-Mastan / $1000-physical split: engineers simulate before they build, because guess-and-check with real material is slow and expensive. Every "Run Simulation" here stands in for a $1 virtual test.
