# Audit of the Cycles reimplementation, 2026-10-15

Purpose: go back to what the paper and supplement actually give us, say plainly where the engine is sound and where it is not, and set a plan with a realistic chance of working. Written after rows 32-41 of `prototypes/REFIT_PLAN.md`.

## 1. What the source documents contain

Main paper (Kemanian et al. 2024): 7 numbered equations. Eq. 1 (capacitance-weighted conductivity, exponent misprinted, `2+3b` should be `2+3/b`), Eq. 2 (travel-time step), Eq. 3-5 (growth = min of radiation- and transpiration-limited), Eq. 6 (double-sigmoid canopy), Eq. 7 (plant-density canopy adjustment). Section 2.6 describes the soil carbon and nitrogen saturation theory in words.

Supplement: 15 equations. SI.1 transpiration, SI.2-7 curve-number runoff (two sign misprints), SI.8-9 shoot partitioning and harvest index, SI.10-14 the two-pool saturation carbon system, SI.15 an RMSE-type statistic. Table SI.2 gives RUE and WUE by species.

Evaluation reported by the authors: daily ET (relative mean square error about 10 percent), willow CO2 uptake, wheat growth and N uptake, soybean soil water. There is no reported evaluation of nitrification, denitrification, volatilization, leaching, N2O or net mineralization against observations.

## 3. Honest assessment

What is good:
- Water, radiation, canopy, partitioning follow the published equations and were verified against Cycles daily output.
- Evaluation is now statistically sound: 268 sites, paired bootstrap, nested calibration, a fresh set. This caught two optimistic results that the old 28-site workflow would have shipped.
- Current standing: regionalized composite error is about 16 percent below the unregionalized engine on held-out sites; c1 adds a small, significant gain at N=400 for corn and soybean.

What is bad or unproven:
1. The target is another model. The authors report no N-cycle evaluation against observations. Calibrated crop models typically sit near 26 percent relative RMSE against measured yields and multi-model spread is near 28 percent. Our N=150 error against Cycles (about 20 to 24 percent of Cycles' mean) is the same size as the uncertainty of Cycles itself. We are close to the point where more agreement with Cycles is not more truth.
2. Regionalization is calibration. It lowers error without explaining it; m and g still vary with soil and climate, which means a missing mechanism is hiding behind them.
3. What students see is not what we validate. The page engines do not use `regional_params.py`; the multi-site accuracy numbers describe the harness, not the shipped engines.
4. The reference sets are being consumed. The 28 original sites have been tuned on for months, the 268 set is used for selection, and the fresh 94 have now informed three decisions.
5. Most N-loss terms were fit by regression on real weather, where temperature, moisture, and pool size move together. The fits are confounded and do not transfer well (Rock Springs-fit denitrification failed at other sites until refit).
6. Only corn and soybean have multi-site references. Wheat and silage corn rest on Rock Springs alone and are weak (silage corn r 0.09).

## 4. What we actually need

Not more single terms. Three things, in order of how much error they carry:
1. A mechanistic soil N supply (net mineralization as a function of SOC, clay, temperature, moisture), so m stops being a fitted regional multiplier. This carries most of the N=0 error (61 percent in the earlier attribution) and part of N=150.
2. Growth and canopy under water stress, which carries the N=400 error.
3. Independent evidence that the response to N is right, since Cycles may not be.

## 5. Plan (ranked by likelihood of success)

A. Designed experiments on Cycles (highest likelihood). Cycles is a deterministic model we can run without limit. So far we inferred its behavior from observational output on real weather. Instead run controlled experiments with synthetic weather and soil: constant temperature and moisture, factorial grids of SOC, clay, temperature and moisture, no fertilizer, one year, reading net mineralization, pool sizes, stress, and growth straight from its daily output. Confounding disappears and each function (fE, fA, net N flux, uptake rate, N stress shape, canopy expansion) is identified separately. Expected gain: replaces the regional multiplier m with a mechanism; target a 5 to 10 percent further reduction at N=0 and N=150 on held-out sites. Contingency: if the identified surface still needs a regional factor, the residual points to the specific missing state (for example residue C:N or pool initialization). Cost: a few hundred short Cycles runs (minutes) plus fitting; moderate.

B. Port and test what ships. Embed the regional model (needs longitude and weather summary at runtime) in the page engines, or state the limitation in the pages. Expected gain: removes the validated-versus-shipped gap. Likelihood high; cost low to moderate.

C. Fresh holdout. Build a new site set with a different seed and reduced spacing, locked until final reporting. Likelihood high; cost low.

D. Independent observation check. Compare the engine and Cycles N response curves with measured data (published N-rate trials, USDA yields). Likelihood medium: access from this sandbox may be blocked, so this may need data supplied by Matt. Gain: tells us whether further matching to Cycles is worth anything.

E. Same designed-experiment approach for N-loss terms (nitrification, denitrification, volatilization) after A, using controlled moisture and temperature steps. Likelihood high; value moderate.

Recommended order: C (cheap, protects everything after), A (largest expected gain, high likelihood), then B, then D if data can be obtained, then E.
