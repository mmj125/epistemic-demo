2. Full simulation controls

Every parameter simulate_season() actually accepts, in one place — including six mechanisms (nitrogen timing, previous-crop credit, manure, irrigation, tillage, planting-date override) that exist in the engine but have never had UI anywhere else in this project until now. Defaults reproduce the plain single-lump- nitrogen baseline used throughout the rest of this project.

Crop & year
Nitrogen
Manure counts at 50% availability vs. mineral fertilizer (Kemanian et al. 2024 SI Sec. IX, a real disclosed number, not invented) — 100 kg/ha of manure N supplies the same 50 kg/ha a mineral source would. Nitrogen has no effect on legumes (soybean, hairy vetch): real rhizobia N fixation, not a modeled gap.
Irrigation
Real Cycles supports irrigation as operation-file-configured (Kemanian et al. 2024 Sec. 2.8: "conditional to soil moisture") but discloses no universal trigger formula — this is real scenario configuration, same as native Cycles requires, not a computed rule either model derives on its own.
Tillage
Applied at planting. Real per-implement depth/soil-disturbance-rating/mixing-efficiency from Cycles v1.4.4's own till.txt (86 real implements, 5 exposed here spanning the real intensity range). Soil moisture is genuinely mixed by the real coefficient; the background-nitrogen boost now follows C-Farm's own real, disclosed decomposition-acceleration formula (Kemanian & Stockle 2010 Eq. 5 -- a cumulative disturbance rating that jumps on tillage and decays daily, rather than a flat multiplier held for a fixed window), still a disclosed stand-in for tillage's real six-pool effect, which this engine doesn't implement (see the gaps section below).
Background nitrogen mechanism
"RothC" is the flat BACKGROUND_N_KG_HA_DAY constant scaled by RothC's own real temperature/moisture response -- the mechanism every crop's own default validation above uses. "Two-pool" is the real Kemanian et al. 2024 SI Eq. SI.10-14 carbon-mineralization structure (built 2026-10-01, see QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md for the full back-calculation trail), now promoted to winter wheat's own default validation above (correlation 0.444→0.490→0.455) after it matched or beat RothC's own accuracy at Rock Springs, Kansas, and Iowa -- still opt-in for corn/soybean here since neither crop's default validation exercises nitrogen tracking at all. One real, disclosed placeholder remains in it (the microbial-saturation factor fA fixed at 1.0, flagged directly in the engine's own module-header comment) -- shown here, not hidden, since this panel's whole purpose is exposing exactly what is and isn't verified. 2026-10-02: the single flat carbon-to-nitrogen ratio this mechanism used to convert ALL respired carbon into mineralized nitrogen (CN_RATIO_SOM=11, a literature guess) was replaced with real, per-pool C:N ratios read directly off Cycles' own disclosed output (soilLayersCN.txt's own real "STAND/FLAT RESID C:N," "MANURE RES C:N," "MIC C:N," and "SOIL ORG C:N" columns, plus a root-tissue C:N computed from CornRM.90.txt's own real ROOT BIOMASS/ROOT N) -- this now correctly models net nitrogen IMMOBILIZATION (residue C:N runs far higher than microbial-biomass C:N, so building biomass out of decomposing residue is a real N sink, not a source, something the flat ratio could never represent). Correlation moved 0.490→0.455, a modest, honest cost of the more physically real mechanism -- still above the 0.444 RothC baseline. See QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md's 2026-10-02 entry for the full account, including a real multi-year-carryover instability this fix exposed (not shipped anywhere, exploratory only).
Denitrification
This engine had NO denitrification pathway at all until 2026-10-02. Rate is back-calculated directly from Cycles' own N.txt output (ContinuousCorn, Rock Springs) -- a daily implied fractional loss (NO3 DENITRIF ÷ PROF SOIL NO3) regressed against soil layer 2's moisture: rate = 0.051717 · theta^4.369. Checked against the real independent seasonal total it should reproduce (annualN.txt's own DENITRIFICATION column, mean 6.62% of applied N across 37 years, range 1.4-11.4%): this mechanism lands at 5.8-10.6% across spot-checked years, inside the real range but not an exact per-year match. See QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md's 2026-10-02 entry and the engine's own DENITRIF_K0 docstring for the full derivation, including why layer 2's moisture fits better than layer 1's.
NH4/NO3 form split
Every mechanism above (denitrification, leaching) applies to ONE lumped mineral-N pool as a disclosed simplification. Checking Cycles' own N.txt directly (2026-10-02) found that's a real mismatch for leaching specifically: NH4 leaching is under 1% of total leaching across the full 37-year Rock Springs record (NO3 43.18 kg/ha vs. NH4 0.39 kg/ha) -- real Cycles treats NH4 as essentially non-leachable, as real soil chemistry predicts (NH4+ is held by the soil's cation-exchange capacity; NO3- is the mobile anion). With this checked, fresh N (an application, background mineralization, manure, a previous-crop credit) lands in a real NH4 pool and nitrifies to NO3 over time at a rate back-calculated the same way denitrification's own was (NH4 NITRIFICAT ÷ PROF SOIL NH4, binned by soil temperature -- a real logistic in temperature, Rmax=0.145/day, reliable only in the 4-17°C range this record's own NH4 pools ever actually occur in). Denitrification and leaching then draw from NO3 only; uptake draws from both. Verified: grain yield is unaffected (uptake/growth dynamics don't change), but leaching drops a real, meaningful amount in a wet year with real antecedent soil moisture (Rock Springs 1996, N=150: 15.95→13.20 kg N/ha) -- fresh N correctly gets a few real days to nitrify before either loss pathway can act on it. See QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md's 2026-10-02 entry.
Ammonia volatilization (continuous, NH4-pool-based)
Replaces, as the better-grounded mechanism, the two approximations under "Fertilizer placement / volatilization" below (a flat IPCC 10% default, and the Macnack et al. 2013 weather-driven one-time estimate) -- both were checked against Cycles' own real annualN.txt VOLATILIZATION column (2026-10-02) and found to overestimate real seasonal loss (4.4% mean, 0.1-8.1% range, 150 kg N/ha broadcast UAN at Rock Springs across 37 years) by 2-8x; Macnack specifically predicted ~37% at Rock Springs' real 2012 planting-day weather. The real process turns out to be structurally different from a one-time percentage lost at the moment of application: it's a slow, continuous, temperature-gated daily draw on the standing NH4 pool that competes with nitrification for the same pool (once NH4 nitrifies to NO3 it's no longer exposed to this loss pathway at all) -- back-calculated the same way as nitrification/denitrification (NH3 VOLATILIZ ÷ PROF SOIL NH4, binned by topsoil temperature, n=13410 real day-samples). A first refit (same session) swapped the unlagged real topsoil column the rate was first fit against for tsoil_lag (this engine's own lag-filtered proxy) -- tested directly whether that basis-mismatch fix would close the gap to Cycles' real total, and it barely did (engine output moved only from 2.0-2.8% to 2.1-2.9% of applied N, still roughly half of Cycles' 4.4% mean). Directly asked next to compare this engine's own day-by-day NH4 trajectory against Cycles' real PROF SOIL NH4 column -- did that, and found the pool trajectory itself is close to a wash (within 4% over the key 20-day post-application window: this engine's NH4-days integral 1668 vs. Cycles' real 1605), ruling out "wrong pool size/timing" as the cause. With exposure nearly matched, the real implied fractional rate itself was the gap: binning the real per-day rate by BOTH temperature and the pool's own NH4 concentration found the rate roughly doubles from low (<20 kg/ha) to mid/high (≥20) concentration at the same temperature -- a real concentration dependence the temperature-only fit never captured, since low-concentration "background" days (the vast majority of a 37-year record) dominated that fit's average. Refit as a genuine two-variable log-linear model (temperature AND NH4 concentration, both real, raw per-day regression, R²=0.59 in log space vs. 0.56 for temperature alone) -- this closes most of the remaining gap: the engine's own 37-year mean is now 5.45% of applied (real Cycles: 4.43%), and the single real 2012 test case moves from an under-3% estimate to 3.97% against Cycles' own real 7.49% that year. Year-to-year correlation against Cycles' real per-year totals is still weak (0.024) and the range still narrower than Cycles' real 0.16-12.13 kg/ha spread -- the mean-level gap is resolved, tracking which years get more or less volatilization is not, the same standing gap already documented for several other nitrogen-pathway quantities in this engine. That NH4-trajectory comparison also surfaced a real, now-fixed bug: this engine landed 100% of every mineral application in NH4 regardless of source, while Cycles' own ContinuousCorn.operation discloses UreaAmmoniumNitrate as a real 75%/25% NH4/NO3 split at application. Fixed via a new MINERAL_SOURCES catalog (17 real products mined from fert.txt, the same way MANURE_SOURCES already was) and a new engine-only fertilizer_source parameter on simulate_season() -- not yet wired into this form's own controls, per this project's standing engine-first pattern. Honest, counter-intuitive result found when testing it against this exact scenario: applying the real UAN split makes the mean-level match WORSE, not better (37-year mean drops to 2.42% of applied N, below Cycles' real 4.43%, and the numbers quoted above are computed WITHOUT it) -- the near-wash in NH4-days exposure the trajectory comparison found was coincidental, two real opposite-direction bugs (this engine's NH4 peak too high from the 100%-NH4 default; this engine's nitrification clearing NH4 faster than Cycles' own does post-application) cancelling out. Fixing one exposes the other, still-open one. Kept and shipped anyway as the physically correct behavior for the real input -- not reverted for a better-looking wrong number. That "other, still-open" bug (nitrification itself running too fast) is now fixed too: re-checking the original nitrification_rate() fit's own disclosed validity claim ("a meaningful NH4 pool never coincides with soil temperature above ~17.1C") against the real data directly found it flatly false -- real samples exist from -13.5C to +28.2C, hundreds per degree through the 20s. The original fit, apparently built from a wrongly-restricted sample, overshot the real rate by ~1.5-1.6x across the 10-20C range that matters most for a spring application. Refit via ordinary least squares directly on the raw per-day samples across their real full range (not a binned-mean fit): the new curve tracks the real per-degree means closely from 4C through 24C. Checked concentration dependence first, the mechanism that mattered for volatilization -- it doesn't apply here; the real nitrification rate is essentially flat across NH4 concentration regimes at a fixed temperature, so the original fit's SHAPE was the problem, not a missing variable. With both real fixes applied together (the UAN split AND the corrected nitrification rate), this exact scenario's 37-year volatilization mean is now 3.37% of applied N against Cycles' real 4.43% (MAE 2.645 kg/ha) -- the best current honest match, still a real undershoot but closing most of the gap the UAN-split fix alone had opened. The no-source default (100% NH4, now paired with the slower, corrected nitrification) overshoots instead (7.28%) -- slower clearance keeps more of the artificially-large NH4 peak around longer. Year-to-year correlation stays weak (0.027), the same standing gap documented throughout this engine. Does nothing (both checkboxes compose to a no-op) unless the NH4/NO3 split above is also checked. See QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md's 2026-10-02 entries for the full derivation.

4. Open questions we asked, and what we found ourselves

  

    
Five specific items were raised directly with the Cycles development team; they were not
    shared. They remain open, alongside additional gaps found independently while building this
    engine. Each is disclosed here rather than smoothed over, in the same spirit as the numbers above.


    

      1. The fwc runoff moisture-adjustment factor (resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Curve-number runoff's soil-moisture adjustment was described only in words in the SI
      ("1 for soil saturated to 0.6m depth... depth-weighted toward the surface"), with no exact
      formula given. Our implementation was a defensible interpretation of that description, not a
      verified match — right order of magnitude, not confirmed exact.

      
Update, 2026-09-22: a separate piece of the same runoff mechanism, the
      AMC-I/AMC-III (dry/wet) curve-number endpoint formulas that fwc interpolates
      between, was upgraded to the exact, citable SCS formulas from SWAT+ documentation (Eq
      2:1.1.4-1.1.5), replacing an earlier rounded NEH-4-style approximation. The two forms agree
      within about 1-2.5 CN points across a realistic range, so this was a refinement to a correct
      formula rather than a fix to a wrong one. Effect on validated numbers: essentially none
      (corn/wheat/silage-corn correlations unchanged to three decimals; soybean's mean absolute
      error moved 0.24→0.23 Mg/ha) — consistent with the earlier finding that runoff
      itself barely triggers before harvest in the Rock Springs record.

      
Update, 2026-09-23 (resolved): replaced our own fwc guess
      entirely with a real, independently sourced formula — SWAT's own soil-moisture-based
      retention-parameter equation (Neitsch et al., SWAT theoretical documentation, Eq.
      2:1.1.11-2:1.1.13), a continuous function of the WHOLE soil profile's actual water content,
      anchored at three real points (an asymptote at the dry/CN1 value, the field-capacity/CN3
      value, and CN=99 at full saturation) rather than an ad hoc 0.6m-depth-weighted guess. This
      sandbox's network policy still blocks the primary source directly (swat.tamu.edu,
      swatplus.gitbook.io, the same class of block already documented for fao.org elsewhere on
      this page), so the equation came from web-search summaries — trusted only after
      reproducing all three of its own defining anchor points to full float precision at Rock
      Springs' real soil profile before using it anywhere. Effect on validated numbers was small
      and mixed (corn -0.003, soybean +0.003, wheat +0.007, silage corn -0.007 correlation) —
      kept anyway as a real, better-sourced mechanism, the same standard already applied to the
      other water-balance refinements above that also moved little or nothing. Worth a final
      cross-check against the primary SWAT document if it ever becomes reachable.

      
Update, 2026-09-24 (fully resolved — Cycles' own formula, not a
      substitute): Matt personally obtained and uploaded two candidate primary sources.
      The first (Stockle, Martin & Campbell 1994, the original CropSyst paper) turned out to
      only cite "the USDA-SCS curve number approach" by name for runoff, with no moisture-
      adjustment equation given. The second — Williams, Kannan, Wang, Santhi & Arnold
      (2012, J. Hydrologic Engineering 17(11):1221-1229) — had it: Eq. 16, a real,
      disclosed depth-weighting function built for exactly this purpose in the same curve-number
      lineage, applied to their own Eq. 11 fraction-of-field-capacity. The paper's own stated
      intent for this exact shape (dividing by depth "reduces the influence of lower layers";
      weighting by layer thickness "gives proper weight to thick layers relative to thin layers")
      matches Cycles' own SI wording almost verbatim ("weighted based on depth, with the soil
      surface having the most importance"). Implemented as depth_weighted_ffc(),
      using Cycles' own stated 0.6m cutoff rather than Williams' own 1.0m (calibrated for a
      different model family, APEX/SWAT).

      
Re-tested the SI's own literal Eq. SI.5-SI.7 structure against the SWAT-sourced
      substitute now that f_wc is real rather than guessed: at Rock Springs, movements stayed

      2. Bare-soil / residue evaporation formula (a real formula found 2026-09-28, not yet validated)
      
Neither the paper nor its SI discloses this equation (the SI's own process flowchart marks
      it as covered; the actual formula isn't there). The potential-demand term (reference ET scaled

Update: the demand term is now modulated by a real, external, standard
      formula for how evaporation slows as the surface dries — FAO-56's own two-stage
      evaporation-reduction coefficient Kr (Eq. 74, obtained via web search since no full Chapter 7
      document has been supplied the way Chapter 8 was) and total/readily evaporable water

      5. Saxton-Rawls secondary hydraulic parameters and real Eq. 1 redistribution (mostly resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Update: Matt obtained and supplied the real Saxton & Rawls (2006) paper directly after our
      sandbox's network policy blocked every attempt to fetch it (including web search, which had
      worked for other sources). All three secondary parameters — air-entry tension (Eq. 4), the
      pore-size distribution exponent B (Eq. 14–15/18), and saturated hydraulic conductivity
      Ksat (Eq. 16) — are now computed in saxton_rawls(), verified against the paper's own

Update, same day: the real Eq. 1–2 redistribution physics itself is now
      partially implemented, not just its input parameters. Re-reading Kemanian et al. 2024's own
      Section 2.4 found that Eq. 1's capacitance-weighted effective conductivity (khe), which looks
      like it needs numerical integration, actually collapses to closed form once Campbell's (1974)
      own power-law moisture curve is substituted in — verified against brute-force numerical
      integration to ~1e-15 relative error. One real correction was needed: the paper's own printed
      exponent "(2+3b)" gives physically absurd near-zero conductivity almost everywhere when read
      literally (checked numerically); reading it as "2+3/b" (a division slash very plausibly lost in
      PDF text extraction) exactly recovers Campbell's own textbook K(theta)=Ksat(theta/theta_sat)^(2b+3)
      form, and was used on that basis.

      
redistribute() now uses this real capacitance-weighted rate (via a new
      campbell_khe() function) in place of the flat Ksat cap described above, when a layer
      carries the needed parameters — a genuinely different behavior, not just a relabeling: a layer
      just above field capacity now drains almost nothing per day, and even a nearly-saturated layer
      doesn't fully drain back to field capacity in one day, matching the paper's own stated intent
      ("weighting by capacitance slows down water flow as the soil approaches field capacity").

      
Update, same day: that first version applied campbell_khe() once per day using
      only the day's starting moisture (a single Euler step), and checking it against finer sub-daily
      stepping found this substantially overdrains — a synthetic near-saturated layer drained 22.2mm
      in one step vs. a converged ~12.2mm once integrated at hourly resolution, roughly 1.8x too much.

      day, recomputing the rate each step as moisture (and therefore the rate) falls — a disclosed,
      fixed-step approximation of the paper's own adaptive-step-size scheme (Eq. 2), not a literal
      implementation of that adaptive stepping, but a real numerical integration of the governing rate
      law rather than one coarse step. 24 steps was chosen after checking convergence directly (1.5%

      6. Every soil layer starts at field capacity, every run — attempted a fix, found it's insufficient

      weather run as uncropped fallow before the tracked season starts) to replace the always-full

      7. Tillage's real mechanism isn't implemented — only a disclosed stand-in for its effect
      
Real Cycles ties tillage to two things: mixing soil layers by a real implement-specific
      coefficient, and temporarily accelerating organic-matter decomposition via a factor in the full

      8. Ammonia volatilization pathway (partially resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Confirmed directly: neither the paper nor its SI discloses nitrification, denitrification, or
      ammonia volatilization equations (only the mineralization/organic-matter side is given, SI Sec.
      V Eq. SI.10–14). Cycles' own real formula for this remains undisclosed.

      
Update: rather than wait on Cycles' own equation, added a real, independently
      sourced loss pathway from the general soil-science literature — IPCC's own 2006/2019
      Refinement Tier-1 default emission factors (Table 11.3): 10% of applied synthetic fertilizer N
      and 20% of applied manure N volatilize as ammonia when surface-broadcast. This is reduced by
      whichever real fertilizer-placement implement is selected, reusing the same real per-implement
      mixing efficiency already parsed from Cycles' own till.txt for tillage (injected/knifed
      placements are physically shielded from atmospheric loss the way surface broadcast never is).
      Previously this engine had no volatilization pathway at all, so injected-vs-broadcast placement
      (which real growers use specifically to cut this loss) had nothing to modulate. Volatilized N
      is tracked separately (n_volatilized_kg_ha) for a complete mass balance alongside uptake/
      leached/remaining, and never reaches the crop or the leaching pathway.

      
Verified: a broadcast application of 150 kg N/ha volatilizes exactly 15.0 kg (10% at zero
      mixing efficiency); the same rate through a real anhydrous-knife implement (26.6% mixing
      efficiency) volatilizes 11.0 kg instead, and yield is measurably higher under injection at a
      nitrogen-limiting rate — the real, physically expected direction. Zero effect on any of the
      four validated crops' correlation figures (the harnesses don't pass fert_placement_implement,

      engine mechanism only, matching this project's own "engine first, UI later" pattern already used
      for the six mechanisms added 2026-09-17.

      
Second update, 2026-09-23: the exact paper behind the IPCC-default
      alternative flagged above turned out to already be sitting in this repo (Macnack, Chim &
      Raun 2013, Applied Model for Estimating Potential Ammonia Loss from Surface Applied
      Urea, Communications in Soil Science and Plant Analysis 44:2055–2063) — a
      composite of 159 records / 43 site-years from 25 published articles (1960–2010), fit as
      three separate linear regressions (ammonia loss vs. soil pH, wind speed, air temperature) then
      combined additively: AL(%) = −40.7 + 8.43·pH + 3.85·WS(m/s) +
      0.33·AT(°C). Added as an opt-in soil_ph parameter that, when given,

      carries), still reduced by the same implement mixing-efficiency for incorporated placements.
      Manure keeps the flat IPCC default regardless, since Macnack's data is urea-specific.

      
Honest limitations, not smoothed over: each individual regression's own
      r² is low (0.04–0.27, real values from the paper's Table 1) — a composite
      built across studies with different, undisclosed measurement windows, not a precise per-day
      flux model. It needs a soil pH input this engine has no real source for at all (STATSGO2
      carries no pH field) — soil_ph is caller-supplied, not derived internally; a
      typical cropland default (~6.5) is reasonable but not site-specific. The linear form is
      unbounded outside the real data's range, so it's clamped to a [0,100]% fraction rather than

      real but high 37% loss on a broadcast application — plausible for this model's own
      inputs, not independently checked against a real field measurement for this exact site/day.

      none of the four validated crops' correlations moved, since neither harness passes it) and
      mass-balance-exact (supply = uptake + leached + remaining + volatilized, checked to 4
      decimals). Still no UI wiring in any panel.

      interception] and [thermal-time fraction], rejuvenating the canopy" (Sec. 2.4) — the same
      category of effect as a cutting event, not a simple growth-rate penalty. Real, per-crop
      MIN_TEMPERATURE_FOR_COLD_DAMAGE/THRESHOLD_TEMPERATURE_FOR_COLD_DAMAGE
      values exist in the crop file and are dramatically differentiated by crop (corn/soybean:
      threshold ~2-3°C; winter wheat: threshold -10°C, min -25°C) — real data, never
      used anywhere in this engine.

      
Checked whether it would even matter before investing further: corn's real threshold is
      crossed on 246 days across the full 37-year Rock Springs record, wheat's on 610 days across its
      real seasons — not a rare edge case for either crop. Searched the general crop-modeling
      literature (STICS, WOFOST) for a portable cold-damage function and found the standard shape is a
      linear/trapezoidal threshold-to-minimum ramp, plus a real "Frost Damage Index" concept
      specifically for wheat with a base temperature of -9°C — strikingly close to Cycles'
      own -10°C wheat threshold, a real corroborating cross-check. But no source found gives an
      implementable formula for the magnitude of the canopy/thermal-time "rejuvenation," only
      its qualitative shape. This is the same open category as item 3 (the pasture cutting trigger)
      — a real, disclosed reset mechanism whose exact reset size isn't recoverable from any
      source tried. Not implemented rather than guessed at.

      17. Radiation-limited growth had no cold-temperature reduction at all — the largest
      single correlation improvement this project has seen from any one fix
      
GenericCrops.crop's RADIATION_USE_EFFICIENCY is explicitly labeled "Maximum eR" in
      Kemanian et al. 2024's own Table SI.2 heading — neither source says what reduces it from that
      maximum to a day's actual value, and this engine had always applied it uncorrected. Diagnosed by
      backing out a day's ACTUAL radiation-use efficiency directly from real Cycles' own daily BIOMASS

      explanation first: feeding this engine real Cycles' own exact FRAC_INTERCEP trajectory in place of

A real, consistent gap exists even on WARM days for every crop (implied/nominal RUE ratio
      0.64-0.75) — tested directly as a flat per-crop multiplicative correction and it made every
      crop's correlation WORSE despite fixing the mean, so it was deliberately NOT implemented and stays
      open (QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md item 9). The gap that DID generalize is the COLD-day reduction on top

      18. The warm-day radiation-use-efficiency gap (item 17's leftover), resolved as a
      post-limitation growth-conversion loss instead of a discount on GR
      
Re-diagnosed the ~25% warm-day gap flagged in item 17 and found the original measurement was
      itself partly an artifact. The implied-RUE calculation there assumed every sampled day was purely
      radiation-limited, but many were actually water-limited or ambiguous in this engine's own water
      balance. Binning the same implied-RUE-ratio data by this engine's own GT/GR ratio (nominal,
      uncorrected RUE) shows the ratio rising smoothly from 0.62 (GT/GR 0.6-0.9, water-limited-leaning)
      to 0.80 (GT/GR > 1.33, unambiguously radiation-limited) — most of the apparent gap was
      water-limited days being misattributed to the radiation formula. Restricting to the cleanest,
      most unambiguous days (GT/GR > 1.5, n=23, stdev 0.039) gives a real, residual, reproducible gap
      of 0.785, smaller than item 17's original ~0.74 but still real.

      
The reason item 17 explicitly declined to implement this as a flat multiplier on GR still holds
      at any magnitude: confirmed directly (not assumed) that discounting GR before the min(GR, GT)
      choice makes radiation the binding constraint far more often (a synthetic test moved corn from a
      real 38-46% radiation-limited baseline to 81-85% under a flat correction), which washes out the
      model's sensitivity to the real, water-driven year-to-year yield variation the whole point of this
      engine is to track. The fix: apply the 0.785 fraction to min(GR, GT) AFTER the
      limiting choice is made, not to GR before it. Since nothing else in this engine's day loop depends
      on cumulative biomass (canopy cover and root depth are pure functions of thermal time; soil-
      moisture extraction depends only on realized transpiration, not on how much biomass it produced),
      this is mathematically a uniform rescaling of the season's whole growth trajectory — it
      cannot change which of GR or GT wins on any day, unlike scaling GR beforehand.

      
Verified directly, not just reasoned: applying it reproduces every validated crop's grain
      correlation to three decimals, completely unchanged (corn 0.547, soybean 0.858, wheat 0.397,
      silage corn 0.512) — only the mean moved, absorbed by re-deriving each crop's
      calibration_factor as usual. This is exactly the fix item 17 left as an open question
      (QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md item 9) and directly closes the gap the "Beyond final yield" table above
      has repeatedly flagged: corn's uncalibrated total biomass error dropped from 32.2% to 7.0%,
      aboveground biomass from 32.7% to 7.6%, both with grain's own correlation completely untouched.
      The remaining ~25%-vs-0.785 distinction (what real Cycles' "Maximum eR" actually reduces to under
      the cleanest conditions) is still not explained by any disclosed mechanism — this fix corrects
      the model's OUTPUT to match, it doesn't identify the real cause, which stays open.

    

    

      19. Wheat needed its own radiation-temperature response, separate from the shared
      NET_GROWTH_FRACTION -- a modest, real refinement
      
Item 18's fix (applied globally, one shared 0.785 fraction after min(GR, GT)) assumed
      every crop's warm-day gap was the same size. Checked directly whether that held for soybean
      and silage corn too, using the same "clean, GT/GR > 1.5" method: soybean 0.786 (n=5) and

      transpiration). The nonzero floor at 0°C (0.202, i.e. 0.257 of the 0.785 plateau) is real

      the same reason `TRANSPIRATION_MAX`/root depth/emergence delay were kept despite modest
      effects: real, sourced, and correctly implemented.

      22. Nitrogen stress rebuilt as a real, day-by-day mechanism (2026-09-25), replacing a
      season-total shortcut — wheat 0.473 → 0.523
      
The nitrogen-adequacy mechanism shipped 2026-09-14 (a single whole-season quadratic-plateau
      supply/demand ratio, computed by a separate _reference_n_demand() function that
      duplicated the main loop's own water/canopy physics just to get a season-total demand number) was
      always a structural compromise: it fixed the day-by-day fertilizer pool's own hard-zero-collapse
      bug, but it meant nitrogen stress on any given day depended on the whole season's total supply and
      demand, not on the plant's own actual nitrogen status that day — and every duplicate-loop
      function is a standing risk of the exact class of bug already found and fixed once this session
      (irrigation silently missing from _reference_n_demand(), invisible until a specific
      combination of features was tested).

      
Found a real, better mechanism via CropSyst's own public source (crop_N_common.cpp,
      mingliangwsu/VIC-CropSyst-Package): N_reduction_factor =
      1-(Ncrit-Nactual)/(Ncrit-Nmin), clipped to [0,1] — a function of the plant's own actual
      tissue nitrogen concentration that day, compared against a critical concentration (already computed
      here via the existing dilution-curve n_critical_pct(), previously dead code, never
      called) and a minimum concentration floor. The Lemaire, Jeuffroy & Gastal (2008) paper the user
      supplied (uploaded to the repo as "plant and crop N status paper.pdf") gives real, crop-specific
      dilution-curve coefficients and a Nitrogen Nutrition Index (NNI=Na/Nc) framework, but no separate
      Nmin curve at all — only a generic, non-crop-specific ~0.8% structural-tissue asymptote (Eq.
      4-6), not what was used. Instead: Cycles' own real, already-available, crop-specific
      N_MIN_CONCENTRATION_STRAW field (0.2% for corn/wheat/silage corn in
      GenericCrops.crop) was reused as Nmin — a disclosed reinterpretation of a real
      number nominally scoped to grain/straw partitioning elsewhere in Cycles' schema, for the same
      conceptual role (a low, mostly-structural-tissue floor) CropSyst's own Nmin plays.

      
Implementation: a new day-by-day canopy_n_kg_ha state variable tracks cumulative
      plant nitrogen content; each day's actual tissue concentration (Nactual) is compared
      against that day's critical concentration at the plant's current biomass; the resulting stress
      fraction multiplies water/radiation-limited growth directly, same as the old mechanism did with its
      season-total ratio. Daily uptake into the pool is funded by the existing marginal-demand-rate
      function (n_marginal_demand_pct()) applied to that day's already-stressed growth, not
      potential growth, avoiding circularity. This let _reference_n_demand() be removed
      entirely (not just superseded) — there is now only one loop, structurally eliminating the
      bug class item 20's own fix had to patch around.

      
Verified against every standing benchmark before shipping: the full 4-crop suite is unaffected
      when nitrogen tracking is inactive (corn 0.550, soybean 0.856, wheat 0.523 itself only differs
      because it now tracks its own real fertilizer input by default per item 20, silage corn 0.518, all
      exactly reproducing the pre-rebuild numbers to three decimals wherever nitrogen tracking was already
      off). Wheat's own validated correlation improved again under the new mechanism, 0.473 →
      0.523 (MAE 0.44 Mg/ha, mean recalibrated to match exactly). Corn was re-tested with
      its own real nitrogen input wired in under the new mechanism too and still does not beat its
      no-tracking baseline (0.5095 vs. 0.550) — consistent with, not contradicted by, the
      already-documented finding that corn's real nitrogen stress correlates only weakly with its own
      yield (-0.21) unlike wheat's dominant driver (-0.894), so corn's default validation path correctly
      continues not to track nitrogen. The standing tillage sanity check (real yield gain at a
      nitrogen-limiting rate, ~zero at a non-limiting rate), the manure-availability equivalence check
      (200 kg/ha manure at the real 0.5 multiplier exactly matching 100 kg/ha mineral), and the nitrogen
      mass-balance identity were all re-verified to still pass, both in the canonical engine and in this
      page's own embedded copy after porting (including a real gap caught mid-port: the embedded copies
      had never defined n_critical_pct() at all, since it was dead code in the canonical
      script too until this fix made it load-bearing, and the embedded CORN/WHEAT
      dicts needed the real n_min_conc=0.002 value added explicitly rather than silently
      falling back to a 0.0 default).

    

    

      23. Water stress and transpiration rebuilt as a real hydraulic-conductance mechanism
      (2026-09-25) — a real, mixed result, kept anyway
      
Kemanian et al. 2024 cites a specific mechanism by name for Cycles' own transpiration/water-stress
      model (Campbell 1985, extended by Jara & Stockle 1998) but doesn't give its formulas — the
      paper only names it. Traced it two independent ways this session: CropSyst's own public C++ source
      (mingliangwsu/VIC-CropSyst-Package, since Cycles shares its biophysical fundamentals with
      CropSyst and unlike Cycles this repo ships real source, not just binaries —
      transpiration.cpp's Crop_transpiration_2 class and
      crop_common.cpp's water_stress definition), and the actual WSU CropSyst
      manual's own "Crop Transpiration" page (modeling.bsyse.wsu.edu, blocked from this
      sandbox directly; retrieved via the Wayback Machine outside this sandbox and pasted in verbatim).
      The two independently corroborate each other's structure (a harmonic-mean root/plant conductance
      split, a real leaf-water-potential stress ratio matching the code's own transpiration_ratio
      calculation exactly).

      
This replaces the engine's original mechanism — a single POOLED root-zone available-water
      ratio (avail water summed across every layer within root depth, divided by capacity) — which is
      the diagnosed cause of a long-standing "root discovery" artifact: a shallow, nearly-dry layer's real
      stress got masked the moment root growth reached a deeper, still-full layer, since pooling before
      computing one ratio can't distinguish "water exists somewhere in the root zone" from "the crop can
      actually use it." The new mechanism instead computes each active layer's own real soil water
      potential and solves for the single leaf water potential consistent with ALL of them and the crop's
      real total root conductance, then extracts each layer's own uptake from that shared value — a
      layer near wilting point contributes almost nothing on its own terms, by construction, regardless of
      how much water a different layer still holds.

      
One real, disclosed gap even in this simpler formula: fl, the fraction of total root
      length in each layer. CropSyst's own exact formula for this (crop_root.cpp's
      Crop_root_vital class) needs density_distribution_curvature and
      surface_density, real per-crop parameters with NO default anywhere in the C++ source,
      in Cycles' own GenericCrops.crop, or in the one further WSU manual page (the "root
      editor") that would very likely carry them — not found despite a real search attempt (both
      direct fetch and Wayback Machine access to that specific page failed). Substituted with FAO-56's own
      real, disclosed 40/30/20/10 depth-quartile root-water-extraction weighting instead, a real, sourced
      approximation, not CropSyst's own exact shape.

      
One further, disclosed correction: the manual's own pasted closed-form solution for the STRESSED
      leaf-water-potential case diverges to 1.5x the wilting potential under extreme demand when checked
      numerically — not physically sensible, and consistent with a transcription error in that one
      line (the same class of OCR/typesetting slip already documented elsewhere on this page for the SI's
      own sign errors). The implementation instead solves the manual's own STATED implicit relationship
      directly via plain algebra, which gives the physically expected smooth approach to the wilting
      potential in that same limit.

      
Result: real, verified, and genuinely mixed, not a clean win. Tested against both
      established benchmarks before shipping. At Rock Springs (the only site with real per-year ground
      truth for all four crops): corn 0.550→0.527, soybean 0.856→0.802, silage corn
      0.518→0.516, and winter wheat 0.523→0.332 — the largest single-crop
      cost. Instrumented every real wheat season under the new mechanism and found water_stress
      reads EXACTLY 1.0, every single day, all 9 validated years — wheat never once triggers water
      stress at this site under its own real LWP_STRESS_ONSET=-1000 J/kg threshold. Confirmed
      this is the mechanism working correctly, not a bug, by artificially drying the soil layers in
      isolation and confirming stress DOES trigger then (0.50 at 5% available water) — real Rock
      Springs soil, under wheat's own real root profile and water balance, simply never gets remotely that
      dry on its own. This flattens whatever (arguably not fully physically grounded) water-driven
      variability the old pooled mechanism gave wheat, costing real correlation even though wheat's actual
      dominant driver is nitrogen, not water (-0.894 vs. 0.359, already established — item 20 above).

      
At the harder, more diagnostic 6-year Kansas benchmark (semi-arid, corn, real STATSGO2 soil, real
      NLDAS-2 weather, real native-Cycles reference runs for 1988/1993/2005/2008/2012/2016) — the
      benchmark this whole rebuild was motivated by — the result is a genuine improvement in error but
      not a clean directional fix: fresh-start mean overshoot fell from 3.63x to 3.24x (2012 specifically
      12.49x→11.84x); the one-year-carryover chained case flipped from a 2.16x OVERshoot to a 0.73x
      UNDERshoot (2012: 6.19x over → 0.28x under) — but mean absolute error actually IMPROVED
      despite the bias direction flipping (chained MAE 0.54-0.63→0.35 Mg/ha), and correlation stayed
      high both ways (fresh 0.973, chained 0.986). The "root discovery" symptom this was built to fix is
      real and reduced; it did not cleanly resolve into "now correctly calibrated," it resolved into "wrong
      in the other direction, by less."

      
Kept despite the Rock Springs cost — a real project decision, not an
      automatic one: the new mechanism replaces a mechanism already known to be structurally wrong (the
      pooled-ratio confound) with the actual cited physics, corn's own water-stress response is now
      genuinely more realistic (varies meaningfully year to year, e.g. a real 2016 minimum of 0.23, not
      just a level shift), and the Kansas benchmark's absolute error improves even though its bias flips.
      Wheat's specific non-triggering finding is logged as a real, disclosed, evidence-backed result (see
      QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md's "Resolved without asking" section, the hydraulic-conductance mechanism
      entry), not smoothed over — recalibrated (calibration_factor
      only, which never changes correlation) rather than reverted. Ported into both this page's own engine
      and engine-demo.html's, confirmed byte-identical between the two files' shared engine
      code afterward (their own WHEAT crop dicts legitimately differ in
      calibration_factor only: engine-demo.html's copy is used solely for the
      fall-planted cover-crop role, never itself validated against real Cycles output the way this page's
      cash-crop scenario is, so it was rescaled by the same ratio rather than freshly recalibrated against a
      scenario it doesn't run).

    


    

      24. A real bug found in item 23's own mechanism during a follow-up expert review
      (2026-09-28): false full water stress whenever the root zone hasn't yet grown past the excluded
      evaporative layer
      
The CropSyst manual's own rule ("no transpiration is allowed from soil layer one, the evaporative
      layer") is correctly implemented in root_length_fraction_by_layer() — it returns an
      all-zero fl array while root_depth_m is still within layer[0]'s own
      thickness, since no transpiring layer is reachable yet. But campbell_water_uptake() never
      checked for that all-zero case before dividing actual_mm / trp_mm_day to get
      water_stress — with every layer's contribution skipped, actual_mm stays
      0.0 and the ratio reports water_stress=0.0, i.e. FULL stress, no matter how wet the soil
      actually is. Backwards: "no root-accessible layer exists yet" should mean "no stress signal
      available," not "worst-case stress."

      
Confirmed real and reachable in the actual day loop, not just a synthetic edge case. At Rock
      Springs (root_max_m=2.0, tt_emergence=65 → ttf at emergence=0.036, layer[0] only 0.05m thick)
      root depth already clears layer[0] by the time eie/TRp first turn nonzero at
      emergence — so this specific bug never triggers within Rock Springs' own validation suite,
      confirmed by re-running the full embedded engine and getting the exact same 2012 corn @ N=650 value
      (9.883807722449967 Mg/ha) before and after the fix. At a thicker-topsoil site it's a different story: a
      real STATSGO2-style Kansas profile (layer[0]=0.33m) keeps fl at all-zero from ttf=0.036
      through ttf≈0.099 — a real 15-20 day window, not one day — during which the old code
      reported the crop as fully water-stressed regardless of actual soil moisture. Fixed with an explicit
      if sum(fl) <= 0: return 0.0, 1.0 guard immediately after computing fl.

      
Honest limitation: could not re-run the full 4-crop Rock Springs suite or the
      6-year Kansas benchmark against real Cycles reference data for this specific fix — this sandbox's
      container was reprovisioned since item 23's own work, and /tmp/cycles-run (the real Cycles
      binaries and reference input/output) along with the Kansas benchmark scripts no longer exist in this
      instance. What was verified instead, against the real embedded engine strings directly (not a
      reimplementation): the Rock Springs null result above; a direct scan of
      root_length_fraction_by_layer()'s output across realistic thermal-time values on a
      synthetic Kansas-shaped soil profile, confirming both the all-zero window and the corrected
      before/after behavior at each point; the standing tillage sanity check (moldboard plow: real yield
      gain at a nitrogen-limiting rate, zero at a non-limiting rate) still passes; manure/mineral nitrogen
      equivalence still holds exactly; and the nitrogen mass-balance identity (uptake+leached+remaining =
      total supply) still closes. Ported identically into both this page's own engine and
      engine-demo.html's, confirmed to grow by the same byte count in both and to remain
      byte-identical to each other apart from the pre-existing, intentional WHEAT
      calibration_factor divergence documented in item 23.

      
Since this bug's practical impact is concentrated specifically at thick-topsoil, non-Rock-Springs
      sites, a future re-run of the 6-year Kansas benchmark should show a further, likely modest reduction
      in fresh-start overshoot on top of the gains already logged in item 6 — not yet confirmed with
      fresh numbers against real Cycles output, flagged here rather than guessed at.

    


    

      25. Wheat exempted from the hydraulic-conductance mechanism (2026-09-28) — a
      targeted reversal, not a bug fix, after a direct "specify exactly what's missing" review
      
Item 23's own mechanism was verified working CORRECTLY for wheat: instrumented every real wheat
      season under it and found water_stress reads exactly 1.0, every single day, all 9
      validated years. Wheat's real root profile and water balance genuinely never gets dry enough at Rock
      Springs to cross its own real LWP_STRESS_ONSET threshold (confirmed the mechanism itself
      is right by artificially drying the soil layers and watching stress trigger then). The problem isn't
      the mechanism — it's that wheat's real dominant yield driver is nitrogen, not water (-0.894 vs.
      0.359 correlation against real Cycles' own stress columns, established in item 20) — and the
      OLDER, less physically precise pooled water-stress mechanism happened to still vary day to day with
      weather in a way that correlated with real yield as a rough proxy, which the physically correct
      mechanism's honest "no stress" answer removes.

      
Fixed by simply not giving WHEAT the two LWP fields — simulate_season()'s
      own graceful fallback (built into the mechanism from day one, for exactly this kind of case) then
      routes wheat through the older root_zone_availability()/water_stress_response()/
      extract_transpiration() trio automatically, no new code path needed. Corn, soybean, and
      silage corn keep the hydraulic-conductance mechanism — none of them are known to have wheat's
      specific "always reports fully unstressed at this site" problem. Confirmed via git history that this
      restores the exact pre-item-23 code path and calibration (byte-identical to commit aed6a20,
      and the fallback path's own three functions are unchanged between that commit and now) —
      correlation returns to the previously-validated 0.523. Not re-run against real Cycles
      output this session, since the reference data (/tmp/cycles-run) no longer exists in this
      container; confidence rests on the code-path equivalence, not a fresh validation run.

      
This does not change wheat's status: 0.523 is still below the ~0.6-0.7 classroom-workable bar, and
      wheat is not offered as a cash-crop choice anywhere in this project. What it does do is stop counting a
      real, mechanistically correct improvement as a net loss for the one crop it happens to hurt, while
      keeping that improvement for the three crops it helps or leaves unchanged.

    


    

      26. The "0.523" figure above was never reproducible (2026-09-28) — found while
      nailing down corn, not wheat, but corrects a wrong number this page used to state as fact
      
Item 25's own "confidence rests on code-path equivalence, not a fresh validation run" caveat was
      tested directly once real Cycles v1.4.4 reference data existed in this container again: a real
      git-bisect, checking out and running each commit's exact code against a freshly-obtained, hash-verified
      Cycles reference (ContinuousCorn's own output matched the documented historical md5
      exactly, confirming the reference setup itself is trustworthy), found that every commit since
      the day-by-day nitrogen rebuild — including the exact commit item 25's own comment names as the
      0.523 measurement point — reproduces 0.411 against this real data, not 0.523. The
      real reference wheat yields match this project's own historical numbers exactly (mean 3.92 Mg/ha to two
      decimals, an implausible coincidence if the underlying data had actually changed), the soil file
      matches SOIL_LAYERS_RAW exactly, and the weather file is the same one ContinuousCorn
      already validated byte-for-byte — so this isn't a data-provenance problem the way an earlier
      session's corn 0.771-vs-0.540 correction was. The code, run against real data it should reproduce, simply
      doesn't reproduce the number this page (and CLAUDE.md) had been quoting since 2026-09-25. Most likely
      explanation: the original "0.523" was measured against a real Cycles reference in an earlier, since-wiped
      container instance whose own setup differed in some now-unreconstructable way — not something to
      keep chasing, since the ground truth it would need to be checked against no longer exists.

      
Separately, and unrelated to the above: while using this same real reference data to check corn's own
      optional nitrogen-tracking path (not corn's default validation, which never tracks nitrogen at all), a
      real, live bug was found in simulate_season()'s shared day-by-day nitrogen mechanism.
      biomass_before_mg_ha = biomass * 10 passed an already-10x-inflated value into
      n_critical_pct()/n_marginal_demand_pct() (both want plain Mg/ha, per their own
      docstrings), and demand_today_kg_ha separately applied the Mg-to-kg/percent conversion
      factor of 10 a second time on top of that. Together these inflated daily nitrogen demand by
      roughly an order of magnitude. Caught by the standard sanity check every other nitrogen mechanism in
      this engine has been held to (a very-high nitrogen rate should converge to the exact no-tracking
      baseline) — corn's optional path failed it outright: 150 kg N/ha and 2000 kg N/ha gave the
      identical, badly-undershooting yield, since demand so vastly outstripped any real supply that more
      fertilizer could never matter. Fixed to use biomass directly and apply the conversion
      factor once; verified N=150 through N=2000 now all correctly converge to the unconstrained baseline for
      a representative year, and that the standing tillage and manure-equivalence sanity checks both still
      pass at a genuinely (re-discovered) limiting rate.

      
This bug does not change corn's own validated 0.527 correlation (corn's default validation never
      executes this code path), but it does change any crop whose validation already wires nitrogen in by
      default — which is wheat, via item 20's fix. Fixing the bug moves wheat's number again: real mean
      4.33 (stale, under the old buggy math) → uncalibrated 6.74 under the fixed math (wheat is genuinely
      less nitrogen-stressed than the bug made it look) → recalibrated to the real 3.92 Mg/ha mean exactly
      → correlation 0.285, worse than both the unreproducible 0.523 and the 0.411 this
      exact crop dict gave under the old buggy math — confirming the demand-scaling bug was not itself
      the reason 0.523 couldn't be reproduced. Wheat's real accuracy remains an open question this session
      deliberately did not chase further: the explicit focus was corn (the "rule"), with wheat treated as the
      already-acknowledged exception, not re-opened as a new investigation.
