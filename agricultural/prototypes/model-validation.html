Agricultural Unit · Model Validation

Full model capabilities, and where it falls short

This page exists for one audience: the real CYCLES developers, and anyone else who wants to check our work rather than take it on faith. It exposes every mechanism our from-scratch reimplementation actually has — not just the two crops shown in the classroom prototype — and states plainly, with real numbers, where it tracks native Cycles output and where it doesn't. Nothing here is smoothed over for a student audience. See CLAUDE.md for the full build history behind every number on this page.

2. Full simulation controls

Every parameter simulate_season() actually accepts, in one place — including six mechanisms (nitrogen timing, previous-crop credit, manure, irrigation, tillage, planting-date override) that exist in the engine but have never had UI anywhere else in this project until now. Defaults reproduce the plain single-lump- nitrogen baseline used throughout the rest of this project.

Crop & year
Nitrogen
Manure counts at 50% availability vs. mineral fertilizer (Kemanian et al. 2024 SI Sec. IX, a real disclosed number, not invented) — 100 kg/ha of manure N supplies the same 50 kg/ha a mineral source would. Nitrogen has no effect on legumes (soybean, hairy vetch): real rhizobia N fixation, not a modeled gap.
Irrigation
Real Cycles supports irrigation as operation-file-configured (Kemanian et al. 2024 Sec. 2.8: "conditional to soil moisture") but discloses no universal trigger formula — this is real scenario configuration, same as native Cycles requires, not a computed rule either model derives on its own.
Tillage
Applied at planting. Real per-implement depth/soil-disturbance-rating/mixing-efficiency from Cycles v1.4.4's own till.txt (86 real implements, 5 exposed here spanning the real intensity range). Soil moisture is genuinely mixed by the real coefficient; the background-nitrogen boost now follows C-Farm's own real, disclosed decomposition-acceleration formula (Kemanian & Stockle 2010 Eq. 5 -- a cumulative disturbance rating that jumps on tillage and decays daily, rather than a flat multiplier held for a fixed window), still a disclosed stand-in for tillage's real six-pool effect, which this engine doesn't implement (see the gaps section below).
Planting date
Default: a soil-temperature trigger validated to 2.65 days mean absolute error against real Cycles planting dates.

      Note, 2026-09-23: aboveground biomass used to be noticeably closer to real output than
      total biomass (11.3% vs. 29.1% mean error), attributed to the real shoot/root partitioning equation
      (Eq. SI.8-11) correcting some of the gap. After refitting that equation's own TTf50
      parameter against real Cycles data (see the gap list below, item 13) -- moving it from a literal-name
      guess of 0.5 to a data-fit 0.32 -- aboveground and total biomass errors converged to nearly the same
      value (30.3% vs. 30.1%). This makes sense: a much lower TTf50 assigns new growth to shoot
      much earlier in the season, so less of the season's total growth is ever attributed to root at all,
      and the AG/total split matters less than it used to for explaining the gap. The gap itself didn't get
      worse in an absolute sense (grain yield's own error and correlation both held steady or improved
      slightly) -- root partitioning simply stopped being the story for why AG biomass looked better than
      total biomass.

Plainly: one of five crops we've attempted currently clears our
    own accuracy bar. Corn (our most-used demo crop) does not. We think the model is still
    directionally useful — it gets the right sign on major effects (drought years score worse,
    sandy soils leach less, nitrogen has diminishing returns) — but it does not yet reproduce
    Cycles' actual year-to-year variation closely enough to stand in for it quantitatively.

  






  
4. Open questions we asked, and what we found ourselves

  

    
Five specific items were raised directly with the Cycles development team; they were not
    shared. They remain open, alongside additional gaps found independently while building this
    engine. Each is disclosed here rather than smoothed over, in the same spirit as the numbers above.


    

      1. The fwc runoff moisture-adjustment factor (resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Curve-number runoff's soil-moisture adjustment was described only in words in the SI
      ("1 for soil saturated to 0.6m depth... depth-weighted toward the surface"), with no exact
      formula given. Our implementation was a defensible interpretation of that description, not a
      verified match — right order of magnitude, not confirmed exact.

      
Update, 2026-09-22: a separate piece of the same runoff mechanism, the
      AMC-I/AMC-III (dry/wet) curve-number endpoint formulas that fwc interpolates
      between, was upgraded to the exact, citable SCS formulas from SWAT+ documentation (Eq
      2:1.1.4-1.1.5), replacing an earlier rounded NEH-4-style approximation. The two forms agree
      within about 1-2.5 CN points across a realistic range, so this was a refinement to a correct
      formula rather than a fix to a wrong one. Effect on validated numbers: essentially none
      (corn/wheat/silage-corn correlations unchanged to three decimals; soybean's mean absolute
      error moved 0.24→0.23 Mg/ha) — consistent with the earlier finding that runoff
      itself barely triggers before harvest in the Rock Springs record.

      
Update, 2026-09-23 (resolved): replaced our own fwc guess
      entirely with a real, independently sourced formula — SWAT's own soil-moisture-based
      retention-parameter equation (Neitsch et al., SWAT theoretical documentation, Eq.
      2:1.1.11-2:1.1.13), a continuous function of the WHOLE soil profile's actual water content,
      anchored at three real points (an asymptote at the dry/CN1 value, the field-capacity/CN3
      value, and CN=99 at full saturation) rather than an ad hoc 0.6m-depth-weighted guess. This
      sandbox's network policy still blocks the primary source directly (swat.tamu.edu,
      swatplus.gitbook.io, the same class of block already documented for fao.org elsewhere on
      this page), so the equation came from web-search summaries — trusted only after
      reproducing all three of its own defining anchor points to full float precision at Rock
      Springs' real soil profile before using it anywhere. Effect on validated numbers was small
      and mixed (corn -0.003, soybean +0.003, wheat +0.007, silage corn -0.007 correlation) —
      kept anyway as a real, better-sourced mechanism, the same standard already applied to the
      other water-balance refinements above that also moved little or nothing. Worth a final
      cross-check against the primary SWAT document if it ever becomes reachable.

    

    

      2. Bare-soil / residue evaporation formula (partially resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Neither the paper nor its SI discloses this equation (the SI's own process flowchart marks
      it as covered; the actual formula isn't there). The potential-demand term (reference ET scaled
      by non-intercepted radiation) is still this engine's own proxy, not a verified Cycles formula
      — Cycles' real mechanism remains undisclosed.

      
Update: the demand term is now modulated by a real, external, standard
      formula for how evaporation slows as the surface dries — FAO-56's own two-stage
      evaporation-reduction coefficient Kr (Eq. 74, obtained via web search since no full Chapter 7
      document has been supplied the way Chapter 8 was) and total/readily evaporable water
      (TEW/REW, Eq. 73, confirmed via search against the paper's own formula). Previously this
      engine's only limit on daily evaporation was the top layer's water content down to wilting
      point, with no memory of how long the surface had been drying — evaporation could proceed
      at the full potential rate indefinitely. Now a persistent depletion state (De) tracks days
      since the surface was last wetted, and evaporation decelerates smoothly once De exceeds REW,
      the real physical behavior FAO-56 describes. REW itself is a single disclosed default (9mm,
      near the middle of the real 5-12mm range Table 19 covers by soil texture) rather than the
      exact per-texture table, which wasn't recoverable from search.

      
Honest result: zero measurable effect on any of the four validated crops.
      Checked why directly: at Rock Springs, real rainfall resets the depletion state before it ever
      exceeds REW, even across the driest 60-day stretch checked -- the same pattern already found
      for curve-number runoff, antecedent-moisture spin-up, and the flat/capacitance-weighted Ksat
      rate limits (items 5 and 10): this site's humid climate doesn't stress the mechanism. Kept
      anyway since the formula is real and could matter at a drier site where the surface actually
      dries out between rain events.

    

    

      3. The perennial-pasture cutting/regrowth trigger (declined by devs)
      
Four separate mechanical hypotheses were tested directly against real Cycles daily output
      and ruled out with evidence (a fixed biomass threshold, a fixed thermal-time-since-cut
      threshold, a growth-rate plateau, and the crop file's own stated 50% standing-residue figure
      read literally). None fit the real per-cut data. Correlation across attempted fixes ranged
      −0.276 to 0.156 — no version has come close to workable. No Python implementation
      of any pasture species exists in this engine as a result.

    

    

      4. Winter wheat's water-stress anomaly (declined by devs)
      
Real wheat yield correlates −0.89 with real Cycles' own reported water stress, confirming
      stress genuinely drives wheat yield in the reference data. But real Cycles reports up to 60%
      stress in years where its own soil-moisture output shows only mild depletion (~83% of field
      capacity) — more stress than any standard depletion-fraction function would produce from that
      little drying. Something in wheat's real stress response isn't a plain soil-moisture function,
      and we don't know what.

    

    

      5. Saxton-Rawls secondary hydraulic parameters and real Eq. 1 redistribution (mostly resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Update: Matt obtained and supplied the real Saxton & Rawls (2006) paper directly after our
      sandbox's network policy blocked every attempt to fetch it (including web search, which had
      worked for other sources). All three secondary parameters — air-entry tension (Eq. 4), the
      pore-size distribution exponent B (Eq. 14–15/18), and saturated hydraulic conductivity
      Ksat (Eq. 16) — are now computed in saxton_rawls(), verified against the paper's own
      worked examples (Table 3: Sand texture reproduces 108.15 mm/h against the table's 108.1;
      Loam reproduces 15.48 mm/h against 15.5, and matches the table's 14/28/46 %v wilt-point/
      field-capacity/saturation exactly).

      
Update, same day: the real Eq. 1–2 redistribution physics itself is now
      partially implemented, not just its input parameters. Re-reading Kemanian et al. 2024's own
      Section 2.4 found that Eq. 1's capacitance-weighted effective conductivity (khe), which looks
      like it needs numerical integration, actually collapses to closed form once Campbell's (1974)
      own power-law moisture curve is substituted in — verified against brute-force numerical
      integration to ~1e-15 relative error. One real correction was needed: the paper's own printed
      exponent "(2+3b)" gives physically absurd near-zero conductivity almost everywhere when read
      literally (checked numerically); reading it as "2+3/b" (a division slash very plausibly lost in
      PDF text extraction) exactly recovers Campbell's own textbook K(theta)=Ksat(theta/theta_sat)^(2b+3)
      form, and was used on that basis.

      
redistribute() now uses this real capacitance-weighted rate (via a new
      campbell_khe() function) in place of the flat Ksat cap described above, when a layer
      carries the needed parameters — a genuinely different behavior, not just a relabeling: a layer
      just above field capacity now drains almost nothing per day, and even a nearly-saturated layer
      doesn't fully drain back to field capacity in one day, matching the paper's own stated intent
      ("weighting by capacitance slows down water flow as the soil approaches field capacity").

      
Update, same day: that first version applied campbell_khe() once per day using
      only the day's starting moisture (a single Euler step), and checking it against finer sub-daily
      stepping found this substantially overdrains — a synthetic near-saturated layer drained 22.2mm
      in one step vs. a converged ~12.2mm once integrated at hourly resolution, roughly 1.8x too much.
      redistribute() now integrates campbell_khe() across 24 sub-daily steps per layer per
      day, recomputing the rate each step as moisture (and therefore the rate) falls — a disclosed,
      fixed-step approximation of the paper's own adaptive-step-size scheme (Eq. 2), not a literal
      implementation of that adaptive stepping, but a real numerical integration of the governing rate
      law rather than one coarse step. 24 steps was chosen after checking convergence directly (1.5%
      off a 480-step reference, vs. 82% off at 1 step).

      
Honest result across both rounds of this fix: three of four crops improved further in the same
      direction as before (corn 0.544→0.549, soybean 0.846→0.854, silage corn 0.511→0.530),
      winter wheat continued moving slightly the other way (0.226→0.216) — a consistent, coherent
      signal rather than noise, not a breakthrough (these remain small movements) but the first real
      water-balance change this week that moves anything at all. Silage corn, the one crop whose weak
      correlation was specifically diagnosed as a water-balance timing problem (item 6 below), got
      the largest gain in both rounds. Wheat's continued slight regression is consistent with its own
      separately-diagnosed anomaly (real reported stress far exceeds what soil-moisture depletion alone
      would explain) — a water-balance refinement was never expected to fix a problem already shown not
      to be a water-balance problem.

    


    

      6. Every soil layer starts at field capacity, every run — attempted a fix, found it's insufficient
      
Update, 2026-09-22: added an optional bare-soil spin-up (real prior-period
      weather run as uncropped fallow before the tracked season starts) to replace the always-full
      reset. Tested at a real semi-arid site (western Kansas) against real native Cycles output run
      the same day: it barely moved the result (2.451 → 2.450 Mg/ha, real Cycles gives 0.137).
      Root cause, confirmed by instrumenting the water balance directly: bare-soil evaporation only
      ever dries the top layer — there are no roots during spin-up to reach deeper — so
      even a full extra year of spin-up left layers 2 and 3 sitting exactly at field capacity,
      untouched. Once a real crop's roots deepen past layer 1 (within the first few weeks), it draws
      on that still-full reservoir underneath regardless of how dry the surface got. Real Kansas's
      actual deficit almost certainly lives at depth, built up over multiple real growing seasons of
      an actual crop's roots pulling water down there — something no amount of bare-fallow
      spin-up can reproduce. At our one real-ground-truth site (Rock Springs, humid Pennsylvania) the
      fix changes nothing at all: correlation for corn/soybean/wheat/silage corn is unchanged to three
      decimal places, since a ~100-day humid winter/spring naturally recharges the profile close to
      field capacity regardless of where it started. Kept in the engine (`spinup_rows` parameter,
      opt-in, `None` reproduces the exact old behavior) since it's a real, disclosed improvement over
      nothing — but closing the Kansas-scale gap for real would need genuine multi-year,
      crop-inclusive state carryover (an actual prior crop, actual root extraction, across several
      real seasons), which is a real subsystem to build, not a patch.

      
Update, 2026-09-23: ported into this page's own live engine (previously only
      in the canonical validation scripts) — `spinup_rows` is now a real parameter this page's
      "Full simulation controls" form can be driven with, not just the offline harnesses.

    

    

      7. Tillage's real mechanism isn't implemented — only a disclosed stand-in for its effect
      
Real Cycles ties tillage to two things: mixing soil layers by a real implement-specific
      coefficient, and temporarily accelerating organic-matter decomposition via a factor in the full
      six-pool soil carbon/nitrogen system (Sec. 2.6). We implement the real soil-moisture mixing
      directly. The decomposition effect is out of reach without that six-pool system (explicitly out
      of this engine's scope), so we approximate its practical effect with a background-nitrogen
      multiplier sized by the same real per-implement coefficient — a stand-in for the effect, not
      a port of the mechanism.

    

    

      8. Ammonia volatilization pathway (partially resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Confirmed directly: neither the paper nor its SI discloses nitrification, denitrification, or
      ammonia volatilization equations (only the mineralization/organic-matter side is given, SI Sec.
      V Eq. SI.10–14). Cycles' own real formula for this remains undisclosed.

      
Update: rather than wait on Cycles' own equation, added a real, independently
      sourced loss pathway from the general soil-science literature — IPCC's own 2006/2019
      Refinement Tier-1 default emission factors (Table 11.3): 10% of applied synthetic fertilizer N
      and 20% of applied manure N volatilize as ammonia when surface-broadcast. This is reduced by
      whichever real fertilizer-placement implement is selected, reusing the same real per-implement
      mixing efficiency already parsed from Cycles' own till.txt for tillage (injected/knifed
      placements are physically shielded from atmospheric loss the way surface broadcast never is).
      Previously this engine had no volatilization pathway at all, so injected-vs-broadcast placement
      (which real growers use specifically to cut this loss) had nothing to modulate. Volatilized N
      is tracked separately (n_volatilized_kg_ha) for a complete mass balance alongside uptake/
      leached/remaining, and never reaches the crop or the leaching pathway.

      
Verified: a broadcast application of 150 kg N/ha volatilizes exactly 15.0 kg (10% at zero
      mixing efficiency); the same rate through a real anhydrous-knife implement (26.6% mixing
      efficiency) volatilizes 11.0 kg instead, and yield is measurably higher under injection at a
      nitrogen-limiting rate — the real, physically expected direction. Zero effect on any of the
      four validated crops' correlation figures (the harnesses don't pass fert_placement_implement,
      so this is purely additive and off by default). No UI wiring yet in any panel — this is the
      engine mechanism only, matching this project's own "engine first, UI later" pattern already used
      for the six mechanisms added 2026-09-17.

      
Second update, 2026-09-23: the exact paper behind the IPCC-default
      alternative flagged above turned out to already be sitting in this repo (Macnack, Chim &
      Raun 2013, Applied Model for Estimating Potential Ammonia Loss from Surface Applied
      Urea, Communications in Soil Science and Plant Analysis 44:2055–2063) — a
      composite of 159 records / 43 site-years from 25 published articles (1960–2010), fit as
      three separate linear regressions (ammonia loss vs. soil pH, wind speed, air temperature) then
      combined additively: AL(%) = −40.7 + 8.43·pH + 3.85·WS(m/s) +
      0.33·AT(°C). Added as an opt-in soil_ph parameter that, when given,
      replaces the flat 10% default with this real, per-application, weather-driven estimate (using
      that application day's own real air temperature and wind — data this engine already
      carries), still reduced by the same implement mixing-efficiency for incorporated placements.
      Manure keeps the flat IPCC default regardless, since Macnack's data is urea-specific.

      
Honest limitations, not smoothed over: each individual regression's own
      r² is low (0.04–0.27, real values from the paper's Table 1) — a composite
      built across studies with different, undisclosed measurement windows, not a precise per-day
      flux model. It needs a soil pH input this engine has no real source for at all (STATSGO2
      carries no pH field) — soil_ph is caller-supplied, not derived internally; a
      typical cropland default (~6.5) is reasonable but not site-specific. The linear form is
      unbounded outside the real data's range, so it's clamped to a [0,100]% fraction rather than
      trusted raw. At Rock Springs 2012 (warm, breezy spring planting day, pH 6.5) it estimates a
      real but high 37% loss on a broadcast application — plausible for this model's own
      inputs, not independently checked against a real field measurement for this exact site/day.
      Verified purely additive (soil_ph=None, the default, reproduces the IPCC-only path exactly;
      none of the four validated crops' correlations moved, since neither harness passes it) and
      mass-balance-exact (supply = uptake + leached + remaining + volatilized, checked to 4
      decimals). Still no UI wiring in any panel.

    

    

      9. Nitrogen is one mineral-N balance, not the real six-pool carbon/nitrogen system
      
Full soil carbon/nitrogen cycling — the paper's own stated core contribution — is out
      of scope for this v1 engine by design. We track a single pool: fertilizer plus previous-crop
      credit plus manure (at 50% availability) plus a flat background mineralization rate, minus crop
      uptake and drainage-proportional leaching. It reproduces real disclosed multipliers where they
      exist (the 50% manure discount, e.g.) but cannot reproduce saturation dynamics the real six-pool
      system would show.

    

    

      10. Curve-number runoff — now wired in, real effect turned out to be negligible here
      
Update, 2026-09-22: wired the existing runoff formula into the water balance
      (added `compute_fwc()`, the moisture-adjustment factor the formula needed but never had; a base
      curve number of 75, a flat default with no real per-site source, same simplification everywhere
      else in this project defaults to a stand-in for missing data). Re-ran the full validation suite:
      correlation for all four validated crops is unchanged to three decimal places. Traced why, not
      just accepted it: the curve-number method only triggers meaningfully above roughly 18-20mm of
      rain in a single day at this soil's typical moisture level, and Rock Springs' 2012 record's two
      genuinely heavy storms both landed after the modeled harvest date that year — real,
      physically correct behavior for this specific weather record, not a bug, just not load-bearing
      at our one real-ground-truth site. Topography/slope still has zero effect on anything (no real
      slope data exists anywhere in this project), but precipitation is no longer sent to infiltration
      unconditionally.

      
Update, 2026-09-23: ported into this page's own live engine (previously only
      in the canonical validation scripts) — a run through this page's "Full simulation controls"
      form now genuinely subtracts curve-number runoff and returns `runoff_mm`, not just the offline
      harnesses. Also carried the real SWAT+ AMC-I/III formulas (see item 1's own update) into this
      page's engine at the same time, since they're part of the same runoff mechanism.

    

    

      11. Two likely sign errors found and corrected in the SI's own typeset equations
      
The runoff denominator and the wet-curve-number formula both show a minus sign where the
      external standard methods they cite, and basic algebraic derivation, require a plus. Implemented
      with the corrected sign in both cases (flagged in code) — worth confirming directly with the
      authors, since we could be wrong about which reading is the typo.

    

    

      12. Tried deriving the water-stress response curve directly from real Cycles output — made things worse, reverted
      
Real Cycles publishes both ingredients needed to derive this curve directly as its own daily
      output: a WATER STRESS column, and per-layer soil moisture. Paired them across the full real
      37-year Rock Springs record (4928 real active-growth days) instead of guessing a shape or
      back-solving through aggregate yield comparisons (which would confound this one function with
      RUE, WUE, canopy timing, and root depth all at once). The best fit was clean and physically
      plausible — essentially a square root, stress=(avail_frac/0.45)^0.5, a 60% reduction in
      squared error against Cycles' own reported values compared to our plain linear ramp. Plugged
      into the actual engine, it made year-to-year correlation WORSE for every one of the four
      validated crops (corn 0.540→0.533, soybean 0.846→0.823, wheat 0.253→0.210,
      silage corn 0.501→0.495) — reverted rather than shipped. Most likely reason: the fit
      was built from Cycles' own real sub-daily water-redistribution trajectory (Eq. 1-2), but this
      engine approximates that with a same-day cascading bucket (item 5's gap) — the two
      trajectories don't move through a season the same way, so a curve tuned to Cycles' real
      avail_frac doesn't transfer cleanly to this engine's different one. Useful negative result: the
      stress-response shape probably isn't the dominant lever behind corn/wheat's weak correlation;
      the water-redistribution physics upstream of it is the more likely place to look next.

      
Follow-up, same day: Matt asked what real modelers would do when their own
      approach is challenged, and separately supplied real source PDFs (Kemanian & Stöckle
      2010, the C-Farm papers) and the real text of a page this sandbox's network policy blocks
      (FAO-56 Ch. 8). The C-Farm papers turned out to cover only the carbon submodel, not water
      balance. But FAO-56 Ch. 8 gave something real and checkable: our plain linear water-stress ramp
      IS the actual standard FAO-56 Ks formula (Eq. 84), not a guess — verified by reproducing
      the chapter's own worked numeric example (Day 3 and Day 10 of Example 37) to the given
      precision. What was wrong was the THRESHOLD, not the shape: FAO-56's real Table 22 gives
      maize/wheat a depletion fraction p=0.55 (threshold 0.45), not the flat 0.50 (threshold 0.50)
      this engine defaulted to for every crop; soybean's real p=0.50 exactly matches the old default.
      Notably, 0.45 is exactly the threshold the earlier reverted power-law fit found empirically --
      that attempt had the right threshold and the wrong shape (a concave curve where the real
      relationship is linear), because a grid search over threshold and power together let a
      better-fitting wrong-shape combination beat a correct-shape, real-threshold one. Implemented as
      a per-crop `depletion_fraction` field (real Table 22 values) rather than a shared constant.
      Result, honestly mixed: corn 0.540→0.544 and silage corn 0.501→0.511 both improved
      (small, real, not a regression); soybean is byte-identical as expected (its real p already
      matched the old default); wheat got WORSE, 0.253→0.226. Kept the real, sourced value for
      wheat anyway rather than quietly picking a better-performing but unsourced threshold just for
      that crop -- consistent with wheat's already-documented, separately-diagnosed anomaly (its real
      reported stress is far larger than its real soil moisture depletion would explain by any
      standard function, item 4 above), not evidence this specific fix was wrong to make.

    

    

      13. Shoot/root partitioning's TTf50 parameter — replaced a literal-name
      guess with a real-data fit (resolved, 2026-09-23)
      
Eq. SI.8-11 gives the shoot/root allocation curve and its shape parameters (fsti,
      fstf) but never states TTf50 (the thermal-time fraction at half-max
      allocation) numerically. This engine had defaulted to 0.5, the literal reading of the
      parameter's own name, since there was nothing else to go on.

      
Resolved with real data instead of another guess: the equation implies the day's
      instantaneous shoot fraction of new growth equals shoot_fraction(ttf)
      exactly, so day-to-day differences in real Cycles' own daily AG BIOMASS/BIOMASS output give
      direct (ttf, marginal-fraction) measurements. Pulled 5174 such points from four independent real
      daily-output files (two separate corn seasons, soybean, wheat) and fit TTf50
      against each independently — every one converged tightly to 0.315-0.335, an ~11x reduction
      in squared error versus 0.5. Pooled fit: 0.3205, rounded to 0.32.

      
Re-derived each crop's calibration_factor afterward to keep mean yield matching
      real output (correlation is scale-invariant under that step, so this doesn't hide anything).
      Honest result: wheat's correlation improved meaningfully (0.253→0.276 from where item 12
      left it, the largest single movement from any water/canopy fix this session), while corn,
      soybean, and silage corn each moved slightly down (corn 0.546→0.544, soybean
      0.857→0.846, silage corn 0.523→0.510). Kept despite the small net-negative spread on
      three of four crops: unlike item 12's reverted power-law fit, this is a direct, strongly
      cross-validated measurement of the real underlying quantity from four independent real datasets,
      not an inferred shape, and the value it replaces was never anything but a placeholder.

    

    

      14. Cold damage to canopy/thermal-time is disclosed qualitatively, not quantitatively
      — investigated, not implemented
      
The paper states plainly that "haying, grazing, tillage, and cold damage reduce [canopy
      interception] and [thermal-time fraction], rejuvenating the canopy" (Sec. 2.4) — the same
      category of effect as a cutting event, not a simple growth-rate penalty. Real, per-crop
      MIN_TEMPERATURE_FOR_COLD_DAMAGE/THRESHOLD_TEMPERATURE_FOR_COLD_DAMAGE
      values exist in the crop file and are dramatically differentiated by crop (corn/soybean:
      threshold ~2-3°C; winter wheat: threshold -10°C, min -25°C) — real data, never
      used anywhere in this engine.

      
Checked whether it would even matter before investing further: corn's real threshold is
      crossed on 246 days across the full 37-year Rock Springs record, wheat's on 610 days across its
      real seasons — not a rare edge case for either crop. Searched the general crop-modeling
      literature (STICS, WOFOST) for a portable cold-damage function and found the standard shape is a
      linear/trapezoidal threshold-to-minimum ramp, plus a real "Frost Damage Index" concept
      specifically for wheat with a base temperature of -9°C — strikingly close to Cycles'
      own -10°C wheat threshold, a real corroborating cross-check. But no source found gives an
      implementable formula for the magnitude of the canopy/thermal-time "rejuvenation," only
      its qualitative shape. This is the same open category as item 3 (the pasture cutting trigger)
      — a real, disclosed reset mechanism whose exact reset size isn't recoverable from any
      source tried. Not implemented rather than guessed at.

    

    

      15. Real per-crop root depth wired in — confirmed correct, a null result at every
      real site checked so far
      
Root depth (how deep a crop can reach for soil moisture) had been a single hardcoded 1.4m
      shared by every crop, when the real, disclosed values differ substantially: corn/wheat 2.0m,
      soybean 1.5m, silage corn 1.55m. Wired in as a per-crop override. The full validation suite came
      back byte-for-byte unchanged — traced this to a real, satisfying cause rather than assumed
      the fix did nothing: this project's own hand-curated Rock Springs soil profile is exactly 1.4m
      deep (apparently why 1.4m was the old shared default in the first place), and every other real
      soil profile checked — the STATSGO2 cell nearest Rock Springs (1.42m), even Iowa's own deep
      prairie soil (1.52m) — caps out well short of 2.0m too. No site this project has real soil
      data for lets a crop's real root system matter yet. Confirmed the mechanism itself works with a
      synthetic test (Iowa's real 1.52m profile, Rock Springs' 2012 drought weather): a 2.0m root depth
      measurably outyields 1.4m (+0.14 Mg/ha) once the profile is deep enough to expose the difference.
      Kept the real values regardless — the same standard already applied to TRANSPIRATION_MAX
      earlier the same day.

    

    

      16. Real per-crop emergence delay wired in — confirmed correct, a tiny effect
      
Real Cycles' own daily output has an explicit PRE_EMERGENCE stage with exactly
      zero biomass from planting through a real per-crop threshold (verified directly: corn's own
      biomass column reads 0.000000 through thermal time 64.47, then 0.001000 the very next day at
      69.997 — a hard cutoff matching the crop file's own real value, 65). This engine's canopy
      formula, by contrast, gives a small but nonzero value even at zero thermal time, so a tiny amount
      of growth was happening before real Cycles allows any at all. Gated canopy interception to exactly
      zero before each crop's real THERMAL_TIME_TO_EMERGENCE (corn/silage corn 65, soybean
      70, wheat 100 degree-days) is reached — a clean fix since both growth pathways are literally
      multiplied by that same canopy term. Effect on the four validated crops: none to three decimals;
      each crop's calibration factor only needed a 4th-decimal nudge. Kept anyway, same standard as
      item 15 above.

    

    

      17. Radiation-limited growth had no cold-temperature reduction at all — the largest
      single correlation improvement this project has seen from any one fix
      
GenericCrops.crop's RADIATION_USE_EFFICIENCY is explicitly labeled "Maximum eR" in
      Kemanian et al. 2024's own Table SI.2 heading — neither source says what reduces it from that
      maximum to a day's actual value, and this engine had always applied it uncorrected. Diagnosed by
      backing out a day's ACTUAL radiation-use efficiency directly from real Cycles' own daily BIOMASS
      output (any day with zero N/water stress and canopy cover in a clean 0.15-0.85 range gives
      dB[Mg/ha]*100 / (FRAC_INTERCEP * solar_MJ) = implied g/MJ, no back-solving through yield needed)
      across the full real record for four crops. Ruled out canopy-cover shape as an alternative
      explanation first: feeding this engine real Cycles' own exact FRAC_INTERCEP trajectory in place of
      its own computed canopy cover still left total biomass running 25-40% high all season.

      
A real, consistent gap exists even on WARM days for every crop (implied/nominal RUE ratio
      0.64-0.75) — tested directly as a flat per-crop multiplicative correction and it made every
      crop's correlation WORSE despite fixing the mean, so it was deliberately NOT implemented and stays
      open (QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md item 9). The gap that DID generalize is the COLD-day reduction on top
      of that baseline: wheat's raw, temperature-unadjusted implied RUE averages under 0.35 of nominal
      across its cold fall-to-spring record, and this correlates far better with each crop's own EXISTING
      transpiration_temp_factor (tr_min_t/tr_threshold_t, pooled
      corr 0.69 across corn/soybean/wheat) than with a thermal-time-style factor (corr 0.53). Implemented
      by reusing that exact factor — already computed for transpiration — as a second
      multiplier on radiation-limited growth (GR), not inventing new per-crop cold thresholds.

      
Re-derived each crop's calibration factor afterward (the mean was already close even before
      recalibrating). Net effect on the four validated crops' correlations: corn 0.544→0.547
      (essentially flat), soybean 0.846→0.858, wheat 0.276→0.397 (the single largest correlation
      jump this project has seen from any one fix), silage corn 0.510→0.512 (flat) — and the
      mean-level match improved for all four without further tuning. Checked separately and honestly (see
      the "Beyond final yield" table above): this fix did NOT close, and in one check even slightly
      widened, the gap in the uncalibrated total biomass this model builds its grain number from —
      a real improvement to the calibrated, reported number, not a full fix to the underlying physics.

    

    

      18. The warm-day radiation-use-efficiency gap (item 17's leftover), resolved as a
      post-limitation growth-conversion loss instead of a discount on GR
      
Re-diagnosed the ~25% warm-day gap flagged in item 17 and found the original measurement was
      itself partly an artifact. The implied-RUE calculation there assumed every sampled day was purely
      radiation-limited, but many were actually water-limited or ambiguous in this engine's own water
      balance. Binning the same implied-RUE-ratio data by this engine's own GT/GR ratio (nominal,
      uncorrected RUE) shows the ratio rising smoothly from 0.62 (GT/GR 0.6-0.9, water-limited-leaning)
      to 0.80 (GT/GR > 1.33, unambiguously radiation-limited) — most of the apparent gap was
      water-limited days being misattributed to the radiation formula. Restricting to the cleanest,
      most unambiguous days (GT/GR > 1.5, n=23, stdev 0.039) gives a real, residual, reproducible gap
      of 0.785, smaller than item 17's original ~0.74 but still real.

      
The reason item 17 explicitly declined to implement this as a flat multiplier on GR still holds
      at any magnitude: confirmed directly (not assumed) that discounting GR before the min(GR, GT)
      choice makes radiation the binding constraint far more often (a synthetic test moved corn from a
      real 38-46% radiation-limited baseline to 81-85% under a flat correction), which washes out the
      model's sensitivity to the real, water-driven year-to-year yield variation the whole point of this
      engine is to track. The fix: apply the 0.785 fraction to min(GR, GT) AFTER the
      limiting choice is made, not to GR before it. Since nothing else in this engine's day loop depends
      on cumulative biomass (canopy cover and root depth are pure functions of thermal time; soil-
      moisture extraction depends only on realized transpiration, not on how much biomass it produced),
      this is mathematically a uniform rescaling of the season's whole growth trajectory — it
      cannot change which of GR or GT wins on any day, unlike scaling GR beforehand.

      
Verified directly, not just reasoned: applying it reproduces every validated crop's grain
      correlation to three decimals, completely unchanged (corn 0.547, soybean 0.858, wheat 0.397,
      silage corn 0.512) — only the mean moved, absorbed by re-deriving each crop's
      calibration_factor as usual. This is exactly the fix item 17 left as an open question
      (QUESTIONS_FOR_DEVS.md item 9) and directly closes the gap the "Beyond final yield" table above
      has repeatedly flagged: corn's uncalibrated total biomass error dropped from 32.2% to 7.0%,
      aboveground biomass from 32.7% to 7.6%, both with grain's own correlation completely untouched.
      The remaining ~25%-vs-0.785 distinction (what real Cycles' "Maximum eR" actually reduces to under
      the cleanest conditions) is still not explained by any disclosed mechanism — this fix corrects
      the model's OUTPUT to match, it doesn't identify the real cause, which stays open.

    

    

      19. Wheat needed its own radiation-temperature response, separate from the shared
      NET_GROWTH_FRACTION -- a modest, real refinement
      
Item 18's fix (applied globally, one shared 0.785 fraction after min(GR, GT)) assumed
      every crop's warm-day gap was the same size. Checked directly whether that held for soybean
      and silage corn too, using the same "clean, GT/GR > 1.5" method: soybean 0.786 (n=5) and
      silage corn 0.806 (n=19) -- both match corn's own 0.785 almost exactly, confirming the shared
      constant is well-supported for all three. Wheat did NOT match: n=117 clean days gave mean
      0.471, median 0.502, stdev 0.256 -- roughly half the other crops' value, and far noisier.

      
Traced why rather than just accepting a lower wheat-specific constant: wheat's real
      growing season (fall through early summer) never reaches the genuinely warm, unambiguously
      radiation-limited conditions corn/soybean/silage corn's own clean samples came from -- every
      one of wheat's 117 clean days fell in November through April, and the implied-RUE ratio
      within that subset still correlates strongly with temperature (corr 0.85) even after the
      existing cold-temperature fix (item 17) is already applied. A direct linear regression of the
      RAW ratio (before any temp_factor correction) against daily mean temperature, using a larger,
      cleaner n=401 sample, gives ratio = 0.202 + 0.0375*tmean -- reaching the shared 0.785 plateau
      at tmean~15.55°C, not at wheat's own transpiration threshold of 12°C (a real, distinct
      threshold that was never independently validated for radiation, only reused from
      transpiration). The nonzero floor at 0°C (0.202, i.e. 0.257 of the 0.785 plateau) is real
      too -- the existing formula predicts exactly zero growth at freezing, but real Cycles' wheat
      output keeps growing at about a quarter of its eventual rate even that cold.

      
Implemented as a second, separate temperature-response curve (radiation_temp_factor)
      applied only to GR, with its own floor and plateau temperature, while transpiration keeps
      using its own unchanged threshold for GT. Defaults to reproducing the existing shared
      temperature response exactly (floor=0, plateau=the crop's own transpiration threshold), so
      this is a genuine no-op for any crop that doesn't set rad_temp_floor/
      rad_temp_plateau_t -- verified directly: corn's reference number
      (10.42557318788082 Mg/ha at N=650) is byte-for-byte unchanged. Wheat's own real values
      (floor=0.257, plateau=15.55°C) gave a modest, real improvement: correlation 0.397 ->
      0.399, and the total-biomass level bias (already substantially cut by item 18) improved
      further, 1.233x -> 1.204x of real output. Smaller than hoped given how cleanly the
      underlying data fit, but a real, verified, disclosed refinement, not a regression -- kept for
      the same reason `TRANSPIRATION_MAX`/root depth/emergence delay were kept despite modest
      effects: real, sourced, and correctly implemented.
