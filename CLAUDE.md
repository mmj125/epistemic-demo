### What is known about the remaining error (details in the plan, newest entries last)
- Bias is negative at 54 to 89% of sites, largest at high-yield sites and at N=400. Forced Cycles states at N=400: the engine's water stress is too strong (6 to 10% of level) plus a harvest index residual of about 2.5%. The water formula itself is right (Cycles' own moisture, canopy and demand reproduce its stress, corr 0.99); the soil water state is 0.01 to 0.02 m3/m3 too dry in layers 1 and 2, built in March to May by soil evaporation about 10% high and residue evaporation 30 to 50% low (Cycles' residue term is stored water, not rain-day interception). The measured evaporation law fixes the water budget but unmasks a mid and late season canopy 3 to 7% too large, so it is not adopted; residue scaling alone makes it worse.
- The 150 to 400 N response is only 63 to 79% of Cycles' for corn, silage and sorghum (the 0 to 150 response matches). Pooled r hides it. Forced states: canopy, water and N each recover 8 to 22 points and together all of it, so it is a coupled shortfall, not one mechanism.
- Compensating errors are the norm: fixing one real error exposes the next (APEX denitrification, evaporation law). Wins came from a missing or wrong structure (wheat clock, legume N path, Pravia constants), not from simplifying fitted constants.
- Practical floor: Cycles disagrees with itself by MAE 0.17 (random 10% soil change), 0.74 at N=150 under a neighbouring weather cell, 1.4 under a neighbouring soil map unit, and by 0.80 within-site yearly correlation between its own water schemes.

9. Cycles is the test set, not the training set (Matt, 2026-10-22). Build each process from the paper, SI, Cycles docs and the published literature; compare with Cycles to show similarity. Fit to Cycles output only when the sources are silent, and label the result a suspect with its test. 10. Circularity rule (Matt, 2026-10-22). Fitting a law to Cycles output and then scoring agreement with Cycles proves nothing. Documented laws are swapped as a package, because one-at-a-time swaps in a system of compensating errors always look worse.

### Growth law (read before touching growth, partition or scalars)
Cycles' growth is simple: the paper's law at scalar 1.0 (crop-file RUE and TUE), the SI Eq. 8 shoot partition with a shared TTf50 of 0.42 for corn, silage and sorghum (fitted on Cycles' realised shoot share, rms 0.001 to 0.005), 40 percent of root growth leaving biomass (`root_retained_frac` 0.6, 5/3 root input to soil), canopy as the paper's double sigmoid (Eq. 6, constants 6, -20, -16, 16 applied to every crop through `CANOPY_SHAPE_FORCE`; `canopy_shape` and `eix` crop keys are dead; per-crop plateau from `CANOPY_FAITHFUL`). TTf is plant age in thermal time over the thermal time at maturity; winter wheat's maturity differs by season (1183 to 3226 C days), so wheat runs a pre-pass for it (`season_tt_end`). Harvest index uses the guide's law with `hi_slope` 1.3077 (an accurate empirical correction, do not refit). With Cycles' canopy, water and N factor forced the engine's above-ground biomass matches at N>=150 and grain is 2.5 percent low. The inferred stage terms (`growth_stage_a/c/tau`) were compensating the wrong TTf50; do not use them.

### The simulator page
`agricultural/prototypes/simulator.html` is the user interface and it never embeds the engine: Pyodide fetches `rhizo_engine.py` and `engine_api.py` from the same folder at page load, and the page calls only `engine_api.schema()`, `load_site()` and `run()`. Controls come from `SCHEMA` and result columns from `run()`, so a new engine option is an entry in `engine_api.SCHEMA`, not a page change. The per-crop configurations in `engine_api.py` (`_CORN`, `_SOYBEAN_N`, `_SILAGE`, `_SORGHUM_OVER`, `_WHEAT`) are copies of the validated ones: after any change to the engine or the harness crop dictionaries run `check_parity_2026_10_19.py`. About panel (what it is, agreement table, observations, limitations, sources, all read from `engine_api.info()`, edit `ENGINE_INFO` there when numbers change) and a run record download (JSON with site, configuration, soil, results); the model is named Rhizo (`ENGINE_INFO["name"]`), the engine file is `rhizo_engine.py` (called `cycles_engine_validate.py` before 2026-10-24; plan, logs, audit and the three frozen pages keep the old name as history). The About panel has no limitations list by Matt's choice; the weaknesses go in the cover email. Options: all five crops, a corn and soybean `rotation` (corn first, fertilizer on corn years only), a cereal rye `cover` group (corn, silage, sorghum), tillage, irrigation, split N, manure. Tested in real Pyodide 0.26.2 (headless Chromium, npm copy of the runtime served for the CDN request; `prototypes/tools/simulator_pyodide_test.py`, `simulator_options_sweep.py`): engine loads in about 8 s, each crop runs 3 to 6 s for 38 years. Open: the page is not linked from the splash page; Pyodide against CPython numbers, a slow or phone device and the live GitHub Pages path are untested; re-run in Pyodide 2026-10-24 after the soybean, wheat clock and wheat N changes: all six crops run (wheat 10.5 s).
