# epiSTEMic

Browser-based computational science investigations (grades 6 to undergrad). Python runs in the browser via Pyodide, no backend, no login. Static site on GitHub Pages from `main`. Pre-funding proof of concept; accounts, LMS passback and any backend are deferred to a future hired engineer. Do not scaffold them.

Full history of every decision through 2026-10-17 is in `CLAUDE_ARCHIVE_2026-10-17.md` (691 KB, search it, do not read it whole). Check it before re-deriving anything old, and treat any number in it as possibly stale until checked against code or data.

### What is known about the remaining error (details in the plan, newest entries last)
- Bias is negative at 54 to 89% of sites, largest at high-yield sites and at N=400. Forced Cycles states at N=400: the engine's water stress is too strong (6 to 10% of level) plus a harvest index residual of about 2.5%. The water formula itself is right (Cycles' own moisture, canopy and demand reproduce its stress, corr 0.99); the soil water state was measured 2026-10-29 (8 sites, corn N=150): the engine is WETTER than Cycles in every layer and month, +0.01 to +0.05 m3/m3 (the earlier note that spring soil is 0.01 to 0.02 too dry is stale), with surface residue carbon 3x to 8x Cycles' (4.2 against 1.5 Mg C/ha in January) before the CERES residue law, decomposition factor 1.2 to 1.5x, and winter respiration 2.3x. The CERES law was adopted to address the stock; water content and respiration after it are not re-measured. The measured evaporation law fixes the water budget but unmasks a mid and late season canopy 3 to 7% too large, so it is not adopted; residue scaling alone makes it worse.
- The 150 to 400 N response is only 63 to 79% of Cycles' for corn, silage and sorghum (the 0 to 150 response matches). Pooled r hides it. Forced states: canopy, water and N each recover 8 to 22 points and together all of it, so it is a coupled shortfall, not one mechanism.
- Compensating errors are the norm: fixing one real error exposes the next (APEX denitrification, evaporation law). Wins came from a missing or wrong structure (wheat clock, legume N path, Pravia constants), not from simplifying fitted constants.
- Practical floor: Cycles disagrees with itself by MAE 0.17 (random 10% soil change), 0.74 at N=150 under a neighbouring weather cell, 1.4 under a neighbouring soil map unit, and by 0.80 within-site yearly correlation between its own water schemes.

### Growth law (read before touching growth, partition or scalars)
Cycles' growth is simple: the paper's law at scalar 1.0 (crop-file RUE and TUE), the SI Eq. 8 shoot partition with a shared TTf50 of 0.42 for corn, silage and sorghum (fitted on Cycles' realised shoot share, rms 0.001 to 0.005), 40 percent of root growth leaving biomass (`root_retained_frac` 0.6; the 5/3 root input factor was removed 2026-10-27), canopy as the paper's double sigmoid (Eq. 6, constants 6, -20, -16, 16 applied to every crop through `CANOPY_SHAPE_FORCE`; `canopy_shape` and `eix` crop keys are dead; per-crop plateau from `CANOPY_FAITHFUL`). TTf is plant age in thermal time over the thermal time at maturity; winter wheat's maturity differs by season (1183 to 3226 C days), so wheat runs a pre-pass for it (`season_tt_end`). Harvest index uses the guide's law; `hi_slope` is the documented Hk = m (1 - Hmin) / (Hmax - Hmin), which is 1.3077 for corn, silage and sorghum, 3.4 for soybean and 2.5 for wheat with the crop file values (class A, found 2026-10-28; do not refit). With Cycles' canopy, water and N factor forced the engine's above-ground biomass matches at N>=150 and grain is 2.5 percent low. The inferred stage terms (`growth_stage_a/c/tau`) were compensating the wrong TTf50; do not use them.
