### Rules
1. No calibration factor that masks a mechanism error. Attribute the gap (force Cycles' states into the engine), fit the mechanism from Cycles' own daily output, keep factors at 1.0. Factors still carried are suspects: silage corn 0.67 was removed 2026-10-18 (factor now 1.0; the multi-site runs are 8 to 19% high at 1.0 but the Rock Springs silage rotation reads +44%, an open rotation or manure mismatch the factor was hiding), wheat `net_growth_fraction` 1.057 with `eix_cap` 0.86 (same pattern as the corn pair removed 2026-10-18), the shared `NET_GROWTH_FRACTION` 0.955 (now unused by the five crops except as a fallback). Wheat 1.15 was removed earlier.
2. No site is special. Rock Springs is a crude crash guard only. Quote only multi-site results: 94 fresh sites (`sitesets/fresh94.json`), 24-site quick set, paired bootstrap, `scorecard.py`. `sitesets/holdout.json` (100 sites) is locked until the final report.
3. Quote N=0 results only from the continuous chain (`LEAD_YEARS=continuous`); short lead-ins over-yield unfertilized corn.
4. Check any figure against code or data before relying on it. Stale numbers have misled us repeatedly.
   Cycles daily rows are END-of-day states; a day's flux acts on the START-of-day state (previous row). Fitting fluxes on same-row states cost the nitrification law 0.16 of R2 (0.83 against 0.99 with start-of-day states, 2026-10-20).
5. Before each substantial step: expected change and why, the contingency, and whether a higher-level diagnostic should come first.
6. Do not repeat the same kind of step after two failures; change kind (attribution, input audit, structural change).
7. Before a new mechanism, read the sources: `GenericCrops.crop` (all fields), the Cycles guide (`git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/PSUmodeling/Cycles`, read `docs/input.md`, `docs/output.md`), the paper and SI (committed PDFs, `git show origin/main:<file>`), Cycles daily and annual output, `ENGINE_REBUILD_PLAN.md`, `REFIT_PLAN.md`, `MECHANISM_LOG.md`. State which were checked.
8. Recommend only what is best, not what continues the thread.
9. Cycles is the test set, not the training set (Matt, 2026-10-22). Build each process from the paper, SI, Cycles docs and the published literature; compare with Cycles to show similarity. Fit to Cycles output only when the sources are silent, and label the result a suspect with its test. Every constant and law is classified in `agricultural/PROVENANCE_LEDGER.md` (A documented, B literature, C documented form with fitted parameters, D fitted form, E assumption); update it with any change and run `agricultural/prototypes/ledger_coverage_2026_10_22.py` (must list no uncovered constants or functions).

10. Circularity rule (Matt, 2026-10-22). Fitting a law to Cycles output and then scoring agreement with Cycles proves nothing. The engine is built from the science (Kemanian and others: paper, SI, Cycles docs, APEX/EPIC and CropSyst documentation, DAYCENT and other published models), each law traced to a source in the ledger. Cycles and real observations are tests. A constant fitted to Cycles needs a labelled suspect and an owner for the source that would replace it. Tolerance for a documented law: adopt it if criterion 1 still passes at every N rate (pooled r above 0.8) and the grain MAE cost is within about 0.05 Mg/ha, and report the cost; do not require a paired-CI improvement against Cycles. Documented laws are swapped as a package, because one-at-a-time swaps in a system of compensating errors always look worse (APEX denitrification exposed the nitrate surplus the fitted law was hiding, 2026-10-22). Goal: what a Cycles developer sees is a model built from the same science that lands close to Cycles and to observations (county yields: `COUNTY_YIELD_CHECK_2026-10-20.md`; add flux and leaching observations).
